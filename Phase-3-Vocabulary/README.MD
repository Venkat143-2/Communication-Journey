# 📚 Phase 3 — Vocabulary

Phase 3 focuses on building a **useful, flexible, and active vocabulary**.

In Phase 1, I learned the foundations of English.

In Phase 2, I learned to control **time, tenses, and sentence structure**.

In Phase 3, I focus on choosing the **right words** to express exactly what I mean.

> **Good vocabulary is not about knowing difficult words. It is about knowing the right word, understanding its context, and using it naturally.**

---

# 🎯 Main Goal

By the end of Phase 3, I should be able to:

* Understand words in context
* Learn new words effectively
* Remember vocabulary for longer
* Use new words naturally
* Distinguish similar words
* Understand synonyms and antonyms
* Understand word families
* Learn common collocations
* Understand phrasal verbs
* Express emotions more precisely
* Replace repetitive words with appropriate alternatives
* Choose vocabulary according to the situation
* Build active vocabulary from passive vocabulary

# 1️⃣ What Is Vocabulary?

Vocabulary is more than a list of words.

It includes:

```text id="y3m1eq"
Word
 ↓
Meaning
 ↓
Pronunciation
 ↓
Part of Speech
 ↓
Context
 ↓
Common Combinations
 ↓
Nuance
 ↓
Usage
```

For example, knowing the word:

> **reluctant**

means more than knowing:

> reluctant = unwilling

I should also understand:

```text id="x5zj0p"
reluctant to do something
reluctant participant
reluctant decision
```

And understand the situations where it sounds natural.

---

# 2️⃣ Active vs Passive Vocabulary

Vocabulary can exist at different levels.

## Passive Vocabulary

I recognize and understand a word when I:

* Read it
* Hear it
* See it in context

But I rarely use it myself.

---

## Active Vocabulary

I can retrieve and use the word naturally when:

* Speaking
* Writing
* Explaining
* Telling stories
* Having conversations

The goal is to gradually move words from:

```text id="ykwfvi"
Passive
   ↓
Recognized
   ↓
Practiced
   ↓
Active
   ↓
Natural
```

---

# 3️⃣ How to Learn a Word

Do not learn a word as a single definition.

Use a complete word-learning model.

```text id="m3i5u6"
1. Word
2. Meaning
3. Part of Speech
4. Pronunciation
5. Example
6. Context
7. Collocations
8. Synonyms
9. Antonyms
10. My own sentence
11. Speaking usage
12. Writing usage
```

Example:

```text id="y8tq6b"
Word:
Reluctant

Meaning:
Not willing or not eager to do something.

Part of Speech:
Adjective

Common Pattern:
reluctant to + verb

Example:
She was reluctant to accept the offer.

My Sentence:
I was reluctant to speak because I wasn't prepared.
```

---

# 4️⃣ Context and Meaning

A word does not always have one fixed meaning.

The surrounding words and situation affect meaning.

For example:

> **run**

can mean:

```text id="2qf7w9"
run a marathon
run a company
run a program
run out of time
run into a problem
```

The word is the same, but the meaning changes with context.

Therefore:

> **Learn words in context, not only from definitions.**

---

# 5️⃣ Synonyms and Antonyms

Synonyms have similar meanings, but they are often **not perfectly interchangeable**.

For example:

```text id="0q0v7p"
happy
pleased
glad
delighted
thrilled
```

They all relate to positive emotion, but their:

* Intensity
* Context
* Formality
* Natural combinations

can differ.

Do not assume:

> Synonym = exactly the same word.

Learn the differences.

---

# 6️⃣ Word Families

Many words belong to a family.

Example:

```text id="6e8w0q"
Decide
│
├── decision
├── decisive
├── decisively
└── indecisive
```

Another example:

```text id="v9j0dr"
Communicate
│
├── communication
├── communicator
├── communicative
└── communicated
```

Learning word families helps increase vocabulary efficiently.

---

# 7️⃣ Collocations

A collocation is a combination of words that commonly occur together.

Examples:

```text id="3g9a8m"
make a decision
make progress
make a mistake

take action
take responsibility
take a break

strong argument
strong possibility
strong influence

deep understanding
deep concern
deep relationship
```

Compare:

```text id="2e1t0k"
❌ do a decision
✅ make a decision
```

Learning collocations makes English sound more natural.

---

# 8️⃣ Phrasal Verbs

Phrasal verbs combine a verb with another word.

Examples:

```text id="x8u7y4"
give up
find out
look after
look for
carry on
figure out
bring up
put off
```

Their meanings are often different from the original verb.

Example:

```text id="v9x2qa"
find = discover a location/object

find out = discover information
```

Learn phrasal verbs through situations and examples rather than memorizing isolated definitions.

---

# 9️⃣ Idioms and Expressions

Idioms are expressions whose meaning may not be directly understood from the individual words.

Examples:

```text id="lqf3ks"
break the ice
piece of cake
under the weather
once in a blue moon
```

Expressions are also important:

```text id="3j5w9z"
That makes sense.
I see what you mean.
I'm not sure about that.
As far as I know.
From my perspective.
```

These expressions help make communication more natural.

---

# 🔟 Formal vs Informal Vocabulary

The same idea can often be expressed differently depending on the situation.

### Informal

```text id="q1v8as"
I need to find out what's going on.
```

### More formal

```text id="t5m7wp"
I need to determine what is happening.
```

Learn to choose words based on:

* Friends
* Family
* College
* Interviews
* Emails
* Presentations
* Professional environments

The goal is **appropriateness**, not always formality.

---

# 1️⃣1️⃣ Word Choice and Nuance

Two words may have similar meanings but create different impressions.

For example:

```text id="xk7w4d"
cheap
inexpensive
economical
affordable
```

They are related, but they can communicate different ideas.

Compare:

> He bought a cheap phone.

and:

> He bought an affordable phone.

The second sentence can sound more positive.

Learning these differences is called understanding **nuance**.

# 1️⃣2️⃣ Emotional Vocabulary

A strong vocabulary allows me to communicate emotions more precisely.

Instead of always using:

```text id="8w5z6v"
happy
sad
angry
scared
```

learn related vocabulary:

### Happiness

```text id="3d7r5j"
pleased
excited
delighted
thrilled
satisfied
grateful
```

### Anger

```text id="8z2v5x"
annoyed
frustrated
irritated
furious
resentful
```

### Fear

```text id="r2n4yc"
nervous
anxious
worried
frightened
terrified
```

The goal is to communicate **exactly how I feel**, rather than using one general word for everything.

# 1️⃣4️⃣ Commonly Confused Words

Some English words look or sound similar but have different meanings.

Examples:

```text id="g7q8x1"
affect / effect
advice / advise
accept / except
principal / principle
than / then
lose / loose
their / there / they're
```

Create dedicated notes for commonly confused words.

For each pair:

```text id="4xq1f8"
Meaning
Difference
Examples
Common mistake
Memory trick
```

# 1️⃣5️⃣ Root Words, Prefixes and Suffixes

Understanding word formation can help predict meanings.

### Prefixes

```text id="h5q3bz"
un-
re-
dis-
pre-
mis-
```

Examples:

```text id="q5n7s0"
happy → unhappy
write → rewrite
understand → misunderstand
```

### Suffixes

```text id="7k2m9a"
-tion
-ment
-ness
-ful
-less
-ly
```

Examples:

```text id="r4t9s2"
develop → development
happy → happiness
care → careful
```

This can help expand vocabulary from words I already know.

* YouTube

```text id="7x4m3n"
  ↓

  ↓
```

# 1️⃣7️⃣ Vocabulary Review System

Learning a word once does not guarantee that I will remember it.

Use repeated review.

```text id="k7n2q4"
Day 1
 ↓
Day 2
 ↓
Day 4
 ↓
Day 7
 ↓
Day 14
 ↓
Day 30
```

During review, don't only ask:

> "What does this word mean?"

Also ask:

```text id="0w7s9k"
Can I recognize it?
Can I explain it?
Can I create a sentence?
Can I use it while speaking?
Can I use it naturally?
```

# 1️⃣8️⃣ The 20 Words Per Week System

The main vocabulary target is:

> **20 useful words per week**

A practical schedule:

```text id="4b7p9c"
Monday      → 4 words
Tuesday     → 4 words
Wednesday   → 4 words
Thursday    → 4 words
Friday      → 4 words
Saturday    → Review
Sunday      → Test + Usage
```

Total:

```text id="0d6c2e"
20 words/week
≈ 80 words/month
≈ 1,000 words/year
```

The number is only a target.

**Retention and usage are more important than the raw number.**

# 1️⃣9️⃣ Vocabulary Selection

Choose words based on usefulness.

Prioritize words that are:

### Everyday

Useful in normal conversations.

### Academic

Useful for college and learning.

### Professional

Useful in interviews and workplaces.

### Emotional

Useful for expressing feelings and experiences.

### Expressive

Useful for explaining ideas and opinions.

### Personal

Words that are directly relevant to my life and interests.

---

# 🧠 Don't Chase Difficult Words

Avoid learning difficult words only because they sound impressive.

For example, knowing:

> "utilize"

is not automatically better than knowing:

> "use"

If "use" communicates the idea clearly and naturally, it is often the better choice.

The goal is:

```text id="h4z8u1"
Precision
   +
Clarity
   +
Naturalness
   +
Appropriateness
```

Not:

```text id="k5m2w7"
Difficult Words = Better English
```

| Remembered | How many can I recall?        |
| Used       | How many have I used?         |

The most important progression is:

```text id="p3q6w8"
Learned
  ↓

  ↓
Used
```

# 🔥 Golden Rule

> **Don't collect words. Build the ability to choose the right word at the right time.**

A strong vocabulary is not:

> "I know many difficult words."

It is:

> **"When I have an idea, I can find the words I need to express it clearly, naturally, and precisely."**
